                               Pairs Trading on Sector ETFs: Method and Backtest Results
           1 Data and setup
           Daily dividend-adjusted closing prices of 11 sector ETFs (XLK, XLF, IYR, XLV, XLE, XLI, XLY, XLP, XLB, XLU, VOX), 2004-
           09-29 to 2026-10-08. All prices are used in logs. The data are split by time: the first 70% is in-sample (2004-09-29 to 2020-02-26,
           3,879 days) and is the only data used to select and fit pairs; the last 30% is out-of-sample (2020-02-27 to 2026-10-08, 1,663 days).
           Nothing fitted in-sample is updated out-of-sample unless stated. Positions are decided at the close of day t−1 and earn the return
           of day t. Each unit of position change costs 5 bp. Returns are per $1 of gross exposure.
           2 Stationarity test (in-sample)
           For every pair of ETFs (Y,X) (55 pairs), regress log prices on the in-sample data and form the spread
                                                    s =lnPY −a−blnPX,             (a,b) = OLS fit.                                     (1)
                                                     t      t             t
           The spread is tested with the Engle–Granger test (an ADF test on s with critical values for an estimated residual). Both orderings
                                                                             t
           of each pair are tried and the lower p-value is kept. A pair is stationary if p < 0.10 (10% level). Example. For XLB / XLU
           the fit gives b = 0.866: the spread is the part of XLB not explained by XLU. A stationary spread keeps returning to its mean; a
           non-stationary one drifts away.

           3 Static-mean strategy
           For each stationary pair, the in-sample mean µ and standard deviation σ of the spread are frozen, and the z-score is
                                                                          s −µ
                                                                     z = t      .                                                      (2)
                                                                      t     σ
           Rule: short the spread when z ≥ 1.5 (short Y, long b of X), long the spread when z ≤ −1.5, and close when |z | ≤ 0.25. The
                                          t                                                     t                           t
           position is set at the close and earns the next day’s return. Daily P&L is the return of the spread position per $1 of gross exposure,
           less a 5 bp cost on any change in position. Example. Short the spread with b = 0.866. If Y falls 1.0% and X rises 0.5% in a day, the
           spread falls by 1.43% (1.0%+0.866×0.5%), a gain of 0.77% per $1 of gross exposure (1.43% divided by 1+0.866). Out-of-sample,
           the frozen spread is tested again with a standard ADF test (Table 2); the portfolio is the equal-weight average of the pair strategies.
           4 Rolling-mean strategy
           Same rule, hedge ratio b and σ, but the frozen mean is replaced by the average of the spread over the last 3 years (the window ends
           at t), so z = (s − s¯ )/σ with s¯ the 3-year average. Example. If a spread drifts away and stays there, the rolling mean follows it
                     t    t    t          t
           and z returns toward zero; with a frozen mean, z stays large.
